Section 125 Cafeteria Plans: Pre-Tax Benefits Explained

A Section 125 cafeteria plan is an employer-sponsored benefit plan authorized by IRS Section 125 that lets you pay for eligible insurance premiums with pre-tax dollars. This means your supplemental insurance premiums are deducted from your paycheck before federal income tax, Social Security, and Medicare taxes are calculated — saving you money on every paycheck.

How Section 125 Saves You Money

When premiums are paid pre-tax, your taxable income decreases. The savings depend on your tax bracket:

  • Example: $100/month in supplemental insurance premiums
  • Without Section 125: You pay $100 from after-tax income (actual cost ~$130 with taxes)
  • With Section 125: You pay $100 from pre-tax income (actual cost $100)
  • Annual savings: $300–$400+ depending on your tax bracket

What Benefits Qualify?

Eligible benefits under a Section 125 plan typically include:

  • Health insurance premiums
  • Disability insurance (STD and LTD)
  • Cancer insurance
  • Accident insurance
  • Critical illness insurance
  • Hospital indemnity insurance
  • Dental and vision insurance
  • Health Savings Account (HSA) contributions
  • Flexible Spending Accounts (FSA) — healthcare and dependent care

Note: Group term life insurance over $50,000 and some other benefits have different tax rules. Consult your benefits administrator.

Tax Implications for Benefits

An important trade-off: if you pay disability insurance premiums with pre-tax dollars under Section 125, any disability benefits you receive will be taxable income. If you pay premiums with after-tax dollars, disability benefits are typically tax-free. Ask your benefits counselor which option is better for your situation.

Enrollment Rules

Section 125 elections are made during open enrollment and generally cannot be changed mid-year unless you experience a qualifying life event. This is an IRS rule, not an American Fidelity policy. Plan your coverage carefully during enrollment.
