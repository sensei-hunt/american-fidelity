American Fidelity Critical Illness Insurance: Lump-Sum Protection

American Fidelity critical illness insurance pays a one-time lump-sum cash benefit — typically $5,000 to $50,000 — directly to you when you are diagnosed with a covered critical condition such as heart attack, stroke, organ transplant, or end-stage kidney failure.

Covered Critical Conditions

Critical illness insurance covers major health events that often require extended treatment and recovery. Covered conditions typically include:

  • Heart attack (myocardial infarction)
  • Stroke (cerebrovascular accident)
  • Organ transplant (as recipient, major organs)
  • End-stage kidney (renal) failure
  • Coronary artery bypass surgery
  • Major organ failure requiring transplant listing
  • Paralysis (loss of use of two or more limbs)
  • Coma (lasting a specified period)
  • Severe burns (third-degree burns over a specified body area)

How Critical Illness Insurance Works

When you receive a covered diagnosis, you file a claim with documentation from your physician. American Fidelity pays the lump-sum benefit directly to you. There are no restrictions on how you spend the money — use it for medical bills, mortgage payments, travel, or anything else.

Critical Illness vs. Cancer Insurance

While cancer insurance specifically covers cancer diagnosis and treatment with ongoing benefits, critical illness insurance covers a broader range of conditions but pays a single lump sum. Many employees choose both for comprehensive protection.

Critical Illness vs. Disability Insurance

Disability insurance replaces income when you can't work. Critical illness pays a lump sum on diagnosis whether or not you miss work. The conditions often overlap — a heart attack may trigger both a critical illness payment and a disability claim.

Enrollment & Cost

Premiums depend on your age, coverage amount, and whether you add spouse or dependent coverage. Enroll during open enrollment through your employer's benefits program. Payroll deductions may be pre-tax under a Section 125 plan.
