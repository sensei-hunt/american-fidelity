Insurance & Benefits Glossary

Definitions of common terms used in your American Fidelity supplemental insurance benefits. Click a letter to jump to that section.

ABCDEFGHILMNOPQRSTUVW

A

Accidental Death & Dismemberment (AD&D)
Insurance that pays benefits if death or a covered loss (limb, sight, hearing) results from a covered accident. See Life Insurance & AD&D.
Accident Insurance
Supplemental insurance that pays cash benefits for accidental injuries such as fractures, dislocations, and ER visits. See Accident Insurance.
Attending Physician Statement (APS)
A form completed by your treating doctor that documents your diagnosis, treatment plan, and functional limitations. Required for most disability claims.

B

Beneficiary
The person or entity designated to receive the death benefit from a life insurance policy. You can name primary and contingent beneficiaries.
Benefit Amount
The dollar amount paid for a covered claim. May be a percentage of salary (disability) or a fixed amount (accident, cancer, critical illness).
Benefit Period
The maximum length of time benefits are payable for a single claim or disability event.

C

Cafeteria Plan
See Section 125. An employer-sponsored plan that lets employees choose from a menu of pre-tax benefits.
Cancer Insurance
Supplemental coverage that pays cash benefits upon cancer diagnosis and during treatment. See Cancer Insurance.
Claim
A formal request to an insurance company for payment of benefits under the terms of a policy.
COBRA
The Consolidated Omnibus Budget Reconciliation Act, which allows employees to temporarily continue employer-sponsored health coverage after leaving a job.
Coinsurance
The percentage of medical costs you pay after meeting your deductible. For example, with 80/20 coinsurance, your plan pays 80% and you pay 20%.
Contingent Beneficiary
A backup beneficiary who receives the death benefit if the primary beneficiary is unable to collect (e.g., has predeceased the insured).
Copay (Copayment)
A fixed dollar amount you pay for a covered healthcare service at the time of service. Different from coinsurance, which is a percentage.
Critical Illness Insurance
Supplemental insurance that pays a lump-sum benefit upon diagnosis of a covered condition like heart attack, stroke, or organ transplant. See Critical Illness Insurance.

D

Deductible
The amount you pay out of pocket before your insurance plan begins to pay. Applies to major medical plans; supplemental insurance typically has no deductible.
Dependent
A person who qualifies for coverage under another person's insurance policy, typically a spouse or child.
Disability Insurance
Insurance that replaces a portion of your income when illness or injury prevents you from working. See Disability Insurance.

E

Elimination Period
The waiting period between the start of a disability and when benefits begin. STD: typically 0–14 days. LTD: typically 90–180 days. Similar to a time-based deductible.
Enrollment Period
See Open Enrollment. The designated time frame when employees can elect, change, or drop benefits coverage.
Evidence of Insurability (EOI)
Medical information (health questionnaire or exam) required to qualify for coverage above guaranteed issue amounts or for late enrollment.
Exclusion
A specific condition, treatment, or circumstance that is not covered by an insurance policy.

F

First-Dollar Coverage
Coverage that pays benefits from the first dollar of loss with no deductible. Most supplemental insurance (accident, cancer, critical illness) provides first-dollar coverage.

G

Group Insurance
Insurance coverage provided through an employer or organization, typically at lower rates than individual policies due to group purchasing power.
Guaranteed Issue
The maximum coverage amount available without medical underwriting or health questions. Available during initial eligibility or open enrollment.

H

Hospital Indemnity Insurance
Supplemental insurance that pays a fixed daily cash benefit for hospital stays. See Hospital Indemnity Insurance.
HDHP (High-Deductible Health Plan)
A health insurance plan with a higher deductible and lower premiums. Often paired with an HSA. Supplemental insurance helps bridge the coverage gap.

I

Inpatient
A patient who is formally admitted to a hospital for at least one overnight stay. Contrasted with outpatient care.
ICU (Intensive Care Unit)
A specialized hospital unit for critically ill patients. Hospital indemnity and some other supplemental plans pay additional benefits for ICU confinement.

L

Life Insurance
Insurance that pays a death benefit to named beneficiaries upon the insured's death. See Life Insurance & AD&D.
Long-Term Disability (LTD)
Disability insurance that provides income replacement for extended disabilities, typically starting after STD benefits end. See Disability Insurance.
Lump Sum
A single, one-time payment (as opposed to periodic payments). Critical illness and some cancer diagnosis benefits are paid as lump sums.

M

Maximum Benefit
The highest amount an insurance policy will pay for a specific benefit or over the lifetime of the policy.

N

Network
A group of healthcare providers contracted with an insurance plan. Supplemental insurance typically has no network restrictions — any licensed provider qualifies.

O

Open Enrollment
The annual period when employees can enroll in, change, or drop benefits without a qualifying life event. See Open Enrollment Guide.
Out-of-Pocket Maximum
The most you pay for covered services in a plan year. After reaching this amount, your health plan pays 100%. Does not apply to most supplemental insurance.
Own Occupation
A disability definition where you are considered disabled if you cannot perform the duties of your specific job, even if you could do another type of work.

P

Pathology Report
A medical document from analysis of tissue or cells, used to confirm a cancer diagnosis. Required for cancer insurance claims.
Portability
The ability to keep your insurance coverage when you leave your employer, typically at individual (non-group) rates.
Pre-Existing Condition
A health condition that existed before your coverage began. Many plans have a pre-existing condition exclusion period (typically 12 months).
Premium
The amount you pay for insurance coverage, usually expressed as a per-paycheck or monthly amount.

Q

Qualifying Life Event (QLE)
A change in circumstances (marriage, birth, job loss, etc.) that allows you to make benefit changes outside of open enrollment, typically within 30 days.

R

Rider
An optional add-on to an insurance policy that provides additional coverage or benefits for an extra premium.

S

Section 125
An IRS code provision that allows employers to offer a cafeteria plan, letting employees pay for eligible benefits with pre-tax dollars. See Section 125 Cafeteria Plans.
Short-Term Disability (STD)
Disability insurance that covers temporary disabilities, typically for 3–6 months with a short elimination period. See Disability Insurance.
Supplemental Insurance
Insurance that supplements your major medical plan by paying cash benefits for specific events. Includes disability, cancer, accident, critical illness, hospital indemnity, and life insurance.

T

Term Life Insurance
Life insurance that covers a specific period (10, 20, or 30 years) with no cash value. See Term vs. Whole Life.

U

Underwriting
The process insurers use to evaluate risk and determine eligibility and premium rates. Group enrollment often has simplified or guaranteed-issue underwriting.

V

Voluntary Benefits
Benefits offered by an employer but paid for by the employee through payroll deductions. Most supplemental insurance is considered voluntary.

W

Waiting Period
See Elimination Period. Also refers to the time a new employee must wait before becoming eligible for benefits (often 30–90 days from hire).
Whole Life Insurance
Permanent life insurance that covers you for your entire life and builds cash value over time. See Term vs. Whole Life.
