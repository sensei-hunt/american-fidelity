Term vs. Whole Life Insurance: Which Is Right for You?

Term life insurance covers you for a set period (10, 20, or 30 years) at a low, fixed premium with no cash value. Whole life insurance covers you permanently with higher premiums but builds cash value over time. Employer-sponsored group life through American Fidelity is typically term coverage.

When Term Life Makes Sense

  • You want affordable coverage during your working years
  • You have a mortgage, student loans, or other debts to cover
  • You have young children who depend on your income
  • Your employer offers group term life as a benefit
  • You plan to be self-insured (investments will cover your needs) by retirement

When Whole Life Makes Sense

  • You want guaranteed lifetime coverage regardless of health changes
  • You want to build cash value as a savings component
  • You have estate planning needs (tax strategy, wealth transfer)
  • You want to leave a guaranteed inheritance
  • You've maxed out other tax-advantaged savings options

Employer Group Life Insurance

American Fidelity's employer-sponsored life insurance is group term coverage. It provides affordable protection while you're employed, with premiums often partially or fully paid by your employer for basic coverage. Supplemental coverage is available at group rates.

Key consideration: group term life typically ends when you leave your employer. Some plans offer a conversion option to convert to an individual policy, but premiums will be higher. Review your plan's portability and conversion options before relying solely on employer coverage.
