STD vs. LTD: Short-Term vs. Long-Term Disability Insurance

Short-term disability (STD) covers temporary disabilities lasting weeks to months with a short waiting period (0–14 days). Long-term disability (LTD) covers extended disabilities lasting months to years, beginning after STD benefits expire, with a longer waiting period (90–180 days). Most financial advisors recommend carrying both.

How STD and LTD Work Together

STD and LTD are designed to coordinate. When a disability occurs:

  1. Days 1–14: Elimination period (you use sick leave or PTO)
  2. Weeks 2–26: STD benefits replace 60–70% of income
  3. Day 90–180: LTD elimination period begins (overlaps with STD)
  4. After STD ends: LTD benefits begin, potentially continuing for years

When You Need STD

  • Planned surgery with 4–8 week recovery
  • Pregnancy and maternity leave
  • Broken bones or joint replacement
  • Short-term illness requiring bed rest
  • Mental health leave (limited duration)

When You Need LTD

  • Cancer treatment lasting 6+ months
  • Chronic back or spine conditions
  • Heart attack or stroke recovery
  • Autoimmune diseases (MS, lupus, RA)
  • Traumatic brain injury
  • Extended mental health conditions

Do You Need Both?

Yes — carrying both STD and LTD provides the most complete income protection. STD covers the gap before LTD kicks in, and LTD protects against catastrophic, career-ending disabilities. Without STD, you would have no income during the 90–180 day LTD elimination period.
