American Fidelity Open Enrollment: What You Need to Know

Open enrollment is the annual window — typically in the fall — when you can add, change, or drop your supplemental insurance benefits without a qualifying life event. During this period, you can enroll in disability, cancer, accident, critical illness, hospital indemnity, and life insurance coverage.

When Is Open Enrollment?

Open enrollment dates vary by employer. Most employers schedule open enrollment in September, October, or November for coverage effective January 1 of the following year. Your HR department will announce specific dates. Watch for communications from your benefits coordinator.

What Can You Do During Open Enrollment?

  • Enroll in new coverage — add disability, cancer, accident, or other supplemental insurance
  • Increase or decrease coverage — adjust benefit amounts and coverage levels
  • Add or remove dependents — add spouse or child coverage, or remove dependents
  • Drop coverage — cancel benefits you no longer need
  • Update beneficiaries — change who receives your life insurance death benefit
  • Switch coverage tiers — move between individual, employee+spouse, or family

Guaranteed Issue During Open Enrollment

During open enrollment, you may be eligible for guaranteed issue coverage — meaning you can enroll without answering health questions or providing medical evidence. This is especially important for life insurance and critical illness coverage, where late enrollment usually requires Evidence of Insurability (EOI).

Qualifying Life Events

Outside of open enrollment, you can only change benefits if you experience a qualifying life event. You typically have 30 days from the event to make changes:

  • Marriage or divorce
  • Birth or adoption of a child
  • Death of a spouse or dependent
  • Loss of other coverage (spouse's plan, COBRA expiration)
  • Change in employment status (spouse gains or loses coverage)
  • Dependent aging out of coverage eligibility

How to Enroll

Sign in to the American Fidelity portal during your enrollment window. Your employer may also schedule in-person or virtual enrollment meetings with a benefits counselor who can help you choose the right coverage.

Pre-Tax Benefits (Section 125)

If your employer offers a Section 125 cafeteria plan, your supplemental insurance premiums can be deducted from your paycheck before taxes, reducing your taxable income.
